## 3. Dynamic Yield Curve Slope & Un-Inversion Engine
* **Formula:**
  $$\text{Spread (bps)} = (Y_{10\text{Y}} - Y_{2\text{Y}}) \times 100$$
* **Dynamic Diagnosis Switching Logic:**
  * If $\text{Spread} < 0$: `🔴 Inverted (Late-Cycle Inversion / Recession Warning)`
  * If $\text{Prior Spread} < 0$ and $\text{Current Spread} \ge 0$: `🟡 Bull Steepening — Late-Cycle Transition`
  * If $\text{Spread} \ge 40\text{ bps}$: `🟢 Normal Upward Sloping — Expansionary`
* **The Economic Insight:** Inversions do not cause recessions; they predict them. The crisis occurs when the curve **un-inverts (Bull Steepening)** because the Federal Reserve is forced to aggressively cut short-term rates (2-year yield collapsing faster than the 10-year yield) to rescue failing credit markets or combat surging unemployment.

## 4. Federal Reserve Net Liquidity Meter
Asset prices do not correlate with GDP; they correlate with central bank plumbing:
$$\text{Fed Net Liquidity} = \text{WALCL} - \text{TGA} - \text{ON RRP}$$
* **`WALCL` (Fed Balance Sheet Assets):** Total assets held by the Federal Reserve (the expanding or contracting base of central bank credit).
* **`TGA` (Treasury General Account):** Cash sitting in the federal government's checking account at the Fed. When the Treasury collects taxes or issues debt to primary dealers and investors, cash leaves private hands and enters the TGA, draining liquidity from financial markets until spent back into the economy.
* **`ON RRP` (Overnight Reverse Repo Facility):** Cash parked by Money Market Funds (MMFs) and GSEs at the New York Fed. MMFs are non-bank financial institutions (they hold no reserve accounts at the Fed). When ON RRP declines, cash does **not** mechanically flow into commercial bank reserves; rather, MMFs reallocate internally into higher-yielding short-term paper (chiefly Treasury Bills $\le 13$ months and GSE discount notes). By absorbing massive Treasury Bill issuance directly from the ON RRP facility, MMFs act as a monetary shock absorber—allowing the Treasury to fund itself without draining commercial bank reserves.
* **The Trend Rule:** When Net Liquidity 30-day delta is expanding ($> +\$20\text{B}$), equity dips are aggressively bought. When contracting, speculative small-caps experience severe multiple compression.

# MODULE 2: Engine 1 — The Yield Stacker (Cash-Secured Puts)

```

┌──────────────────────────────────────┐      ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│        COLLATERAL FOUNDATION         │  +   │         SYNTHETIC OPTION PUT         │

└──────────────────────────────────────┘      └──────────────────────────────────────┘
                                          ▼
```

### 1. The Strategy Mandate
We sell out-of-the-money Cash-Secured Puts (CSPs) **exclusively on companies that have already passed our forensic balance-sheet screens**. 
* We demand elevated Implied Volatility ($\text{Absolute IV} > 30\%$) so buyers pay inflated insurance premiums.
* We select Delta between $-0.10$ and $-0.15$ (~85% to 90% statistical probability of expiring worthless).
* Collateral is **never left uninvested**. It sits in 4-week to 13-week U.S. Treasury Bills earning risk-free interest.

### 2. The Core Mathematical Formulas
$$\text{Cash Collateral Required} = 100 \times K \quad (K = \text{Strike Price})$$
$$\text{Premium Yield} = \frac{P_{\text{bid}}}{K} \quad (P_{\text{bid}} = \text{Exchange Bid Price, NEVER Midpoint})$$
$$\text{Annualized Option Yield} = \left( \frac{P_{\text{bid}}}{K} \right) \times \left( \frac{365}{\text{DTE}} \right)$$
$$\text{Stacked Annual Yield} = \text{Annualized Option Yield} + r_{\text{Treasury}}$$

### 3. Asymmetric Payoff Walkthrough (`INTU` Example)
* Spot Price: $\$334.36$ | Strike: $\$280.00$ ($16.3\%$ OTM) | DTE: $37$ days | Bid: $\$3.90$ | T-Bill: $4.50\%$
* Option Return: $\frac{\$3.90}{\$280.00} = 1.393\%$ in 37 days.
* Annualized Option Return: $1.393\% \times \frac{365}{37} = 13.74\%$.
* **Stacked Annual Yield:** $13.74\% + 4.50\% = \mathbf{18.24\%}$.
* **The Two Outcomes:**
  * *Outcome 1 (Stock stays flat, rallies, or drops slightly):* Put expires worthless. You keep the $\$390$ premium plus $\$127$ in Treasury interest. Net gain: $\$517$ on $\$28,000$ in 37 days (18.24% annualized).
  * *Outcome 2 (Stock plunges below $280):* You are assigned shares at a net cost basis of $\$280.00 - \$3.90 = \mathbf{\$276.10}$ (a **$17.4\%$ discount** to the original spot price), backed by cash already in your account.

### 1. The Strategy Mandate

### 2. Core Metrics & Definitions
* **Confluence ($\ge 2$):** The company is an essential supplier to two or more distinct mega-trend anchors simultaneously.
* **Whale Stealth Score ($0–100$):** Measures institutional accumulation using the 20-day polynomial slope of On-Balance Volume (OBV). Scores $\ge 70$ reveal aggressive dark pool absorption without price breakout.
* **`🟢 COILED` Status:** Confirms the stock is pinned in the bottom of its 52-week range ($\le 0.35$) while institutional accumulation volume is expanding (a compressed spring).
* **Elastic Disparity:** The spread between the anchor's 1-year trailing gain and the supplier's trailing performance:
  $$\text{Elastic Disparity} = \text{Anchor Return}_{1\text{Y}} - \text{Supplier Return}_{1\text{Y}}$$

### 3. The Transmission Haircut: Addressing Overpromising
> [!WARNING]
> **Charlie Munger's Law of Supplier Economics:** A component supplier is rarely a 1:1 substitute for a monopoly anchor. Component suppliers have lower pricing power and customer concentration risk.
> 
> *The Quantitative Adjustment:* Instead of assuming the supplier catches up 100% to the single highest-performing anchor, we apply a **Supplier Transmission Factor ($\beta_{\text{sc}} \approx 0.35 - 0.50$)** against the **Blended Average** of its anchor customers:
> $$\text{Realistic Target Catch-Up} = \beta_{\text{sc}} \times \left( \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \text{Anchor Return}_i \right)$$
> Elastic Disparity is an indicator of **asymmetric coiled tension**, not a guaranteed price target.

## 3. The Forensic Accounting Scorecard

### A. Piotroski F-Score ($0 \text{ to } 9$)
Nine binary accounting checks across three core dimensions:
* **Profitability:**
  1. $ROA > 0$: $\frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Total Assets}} > 0$
  2. $CFO > 0$: Operating Cash Flow $> 0$
  3. $\Delta ROA > 0$: Current year ROA higher than prior year
  4. $\text{Accrual} > 0$: $CFO > \text{Net Income}$ (Cash earnings exceed accounting accruals)
* **Leverage & Liquidity:**
  5. $\Delta \text{Leverage} < 0$: Long-term debt ratio decreased
  6. $\Delta \text{Liquidity} > 0$: Current ratio increased
  7. $\text{No Dilution}$: Diluted shares did not expand
* **Operating Efficiency:**
  8. $\Delta \text{Gross Margin} > 0$: Gross margin expanded
  9. $\Delta \text{Asset Turnover} > 0$: $\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ increased

### B. Altman Z-Score (Solvency & Bankruptcy Risk)
$$Z = 1.2 X_1 + 1.4 X_2 + 3.3 X_3 + 0.6 X_4 + 0.999 X_5$$
* $X_1 = \frac{\text{Working Capital}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ (Liquidity buffer)
* $X_2 = \frac{\text{Retained Earnings}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ (Cumulative self-financing history)
* $X_3 = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ (True operating return on assets)
* $X_4 = \frac{\text{Market Cap}}{\text{Total Liabilities}}$ (Equity cushion before insolvency)
* $X_5 = \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Total Assets}}$ (Asset efficiency)
* **Gates:** Safe $> 2.99$ | Grey $1.81–2.99$ | Distress $< 1.81$.

### C. Beneish M-Score (Earnings Manipulation Probability)
$$\begin{aligned}
M = &-4.84 + 0.920 \cdot \text{DSRI} + 0.528 \cdot \text{GMI} + 0.404 \cdot \text{AQI} + 0.892 \cdot \text{SGI} \\
&+ 0.115 \cdot \text{DEPI} - 0.172 \cdot \text{SGAI} + 4.037 \cdot \text{TATA} + 0.0327 \cdot \text{LVGI}
\end{aligned}$$
* Evaluates receivables bloat ($\text{DSRI}$), gross margin deterioration ($\text{GMI}$), capitalization of expenses ($\text{AQI}$), and accruals ($\text{TATA}$).
* **Gate:** Must be $\le -1.78$. Scores above $-1.78$ indicate high mathematical likelihood of accounting manipulation.

### D. Stock-Based Compensation as % of OCF
$$\text{SBC \% of OCF} = \frac{\text{TTM Stock-Based Compensation}}{\text{TTM Operating Cash Flow}} \times 100 \le 30.0\%$$

## 1. ADDV Liquidity Guardrail
* **Formula:**
  $$\text{ADDV}_{30\text{d}} = \frac{1}{30} \sum_{i=1}^{30} (\text{Volume}_i \times \text{Close}_i)$$

* **The Rule:** No position may exceed 2.0% of 30-day Average Daily Dollar Volume. This guarantees that if the thesis breaks, the fund can exit 100% of the position in 1–2 trading days without moving the tape.
